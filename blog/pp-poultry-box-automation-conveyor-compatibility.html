Spec'ing PP Poultry Boxes for Automated Conveyor and Washer Lines

Poultryboxes Editorial • November 2026

Why Automation Changes the Spec

A poultry box that performs well in manual handling can jam, tip, or stall on an automated line. As processing plants add conveyor transport, robotic stackers, and tunnel washers, the box itself becomes a machine component with tolerances as demanding as any other part of the line. Procurement teams that spec boxes purely on capacity and price often discover expensive throughput losses once the equipment is running. Automation compatibility belongs in the specification from the start.

The core issue is dimensional consistency. Injection-molded or extruded PP boxes hold tight, repeatable tolerances batch after batch, which is exactly what conveyor guides, lift mechanisms, and stacker heads require. Boxes with inconsistent footprints or warped bases catch on transfer points and misalign in stackers, creating the jams that halt an entire line. Specifying a manufacturer with proven dimensional control is the first automation decision.

Base and Rim Geometry for Transport

On powered roller and belt conveyors, the box base does the work. A flat, ribbed base with a consistent contact pattern tracks straight and transfers cleanly across gaps. Smooth continuous bottom edges prevent the snagging that occurs when open-grid bases catch on roller leading edges. For plants running chain or slat conveyors, confirm the base rib spacing clears the chain pitch so boxes ride smoothly rather than vibrating.

Rim design governs stacking automation. Interlocking rims that self-center during drop-stacking let robotic heads place boxes quickly without precise alignment, while a defined lip gives suction and clamp end-effectors a reliable grip surface. The PP poultry boxes from Elipacko are produced with consistent rim and base geometry suited to automated stacking and conveyor transport, reducing the misfeeds that erode line uptime.

Surviving the Tunnel Washer

Automated tunnel washers subject boxes to high-pressure jets, caustic detergents, and sustained heat on every cycle. Polypropylene handles this environment without the warping, water absorption, or coating failure that degrades wood and fiber alternatives. Key wash-line spec points include:

  • Heat-stable resin: Maintains shape through repeated hot-wash cycles so boxes stay square and conveyor-compatible over their service life.
  • Self-draining geometry: Sloped bases and drainage slots clear wash water before boxes exit the tunnel, preventing carryover and speeding dry-off.
  • Smooth radiused interiors: Eliminate crevices where organic residue survives the wash, supporting consistent sanitation verification.
  • Chemical resistance: PP tolerates the caustic and sanitizing agents standard in poultry plant wash chemistry.

Load Rating Under Dynamic Conditions

Static load ratings understate what an automated line demands. Boxes experience dynamic loads during accumulation, indexing, and drop-stacking that exceed the steady-state figures in a catalog. Request the manufacturer's data for stacked load under motion and for the impact tolerance at the drop heights your stacker uses. Specifying to the dynamic condition, not the static one, prevents the cracked corners and collapsed stacks that create downstream jams.
